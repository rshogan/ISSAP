# The Chronicle of Cybersecuratia

*Why eight wizards are waiting for you at the edge of a ruined world.*

This is the backstory for **ISSAP Cyber Redemption**, a gamified practice test for the ISSAP exam. Everything below is in-world, but the mechanics it describes are real ones you meet in the game. The same text is readable in-game from the title screen under **Lore**.

---

## The World

Cybersecuratia was built well, once. Its four great nations ran on trusted ledgers, clean blueprints, quiet infrastructure and names that meant something. Nobody thought much about the foundations, which is exactly how foundations prefer to be treated.

Then the colossi came. Four of them, each one a machine the size of a mountain range, and each one built out of a mistake the nation had made and then forgotten about. They did not break in. They walked through gaps that had been left open for years.

What is left of the world is four countries, ninety-odd ruined cities, and eight wizards who refuse to leave. You are the traveller they have been waiting for.

## The Journey

Six beats, mapped onto the shape of the game itself.

### 1. The Call

You arrive with no reputation and no map. A save slot is opened in your name. The wizards have seen travellers before; none of them made it past the second country. They are willing to be surprised.

### 2. Crossing the Threshold

The World Map shows four occupied nations — Governance, Architecture Modeling, Infrastructure, and Identity. Each is a domain of the ISSAP Common Body of Knowledge, and each is held by its own colossus. You may enter them in any order. None of them will be easy.

### 3. The Road of Trials

Every city in a nation is a set of questions that the city itself got wrong. Answer them and the streetlights come back on, block by block, until the whole city is lit and liberated. A wizard walks each city with you and explains every answer — including the ones you get right, because knowing why you were right is the whole point.

### 4. Meeting the Mentors

Eight wizards, eight schools of magic, eight secure design principles. They are not here to do the remediating for you. They are here to hand over everything they know before you reach the thing at the end of the road.

### 5. The Ordeal

Liberate every city in a nation and its fortresses unseal — the hardest questions that country has, no hints left and no easy blocks. Remediate every fortress and the colossus itself finally turns to face you. It fights with what it took from you: at least half of that last confrontation is drawn from questions you have already answered wrong somewhere in that nation, handed back to you one at a time. Put it down and the nation is Liberated.

### 6. The Return

Four colossi remediated and Cybersecuratia is free again. The wizards will tell you that the real test is the one you sit afterwards, in a quiet room, with a number at the end of it. They are not being poetic. That is literally the point of all this.

## The Four Colossi

Each colossus is a threat actor that took a nation by walking through what that nation never got around to fixing. On the horizon of every city you visit, the one holding that country is watching you get closer.

| Colossus | Epithet | Nation it holds | Domain |

| **GOVERNAX** | *The Unwritten Policy* | Governance, Risk, and Compliance | D1 |
| **MODELBREAKER** | *The Broken Blueprint* | Security Architecture Modeling | D2 |
| **GRIDFALL** | *The Shattered Perimeter* | Infrastructure and System Security Architecture | D3 |
| **NULLCRED** | *The Stolen Name* | Identity and Access Management Architecture | D4 |

### GOVERNAX — The Unwritten Policy

**Holds:** Governance, Risk, and Compliance (D1)

Assembled out of every risk nobody agreed to own. It took the capital during a meeting that was rescheduled four times. Its armor is an audit finding that was accepted, deferred, and then never looked at again.

### MODELBREAKER — The Broken Blueprint

**Holds:** Security Architecture Modeling (D2)

Born from a design that was approved without ever being drawn. It does not smash walls; it finds the one place where the diagram and the deployment disagree, and it steps through there.

### GRIDFALL — The Shattered Perimeter

**Holds:** Infrastructure and System Security Architecture (D3)

It walked in on a flat network and never had to stop walking. Every unsegmented link, every trusted-by-default hop, every appliance left on its shipping password became another kilometre of road for it.

### NULLCRED — The Stolen Name

**Holds:** Identity and Access Management Architecture (D4)

It owns no identity of its own. It wears yours. It was granted everything it has, one over-broad entitlement at a time, and every credential it holds was issued correctly to somebody who no longer works here.

## The Eight Wizards

Eight cybersecurity wizards survived the fall. Each one mastered a single secure design principle so completely that it became a school of magic — and each one is convinced their principle is the one that would have saved the world, had anyone listened.

Each wizard is one of the eight classic secure design principles, themed on one of the eight schools of magic. A wizard is assigned per city and stays with you for every question in it, explaining every answer — including the ones you get right.

### Zephyrine the Abjurist

**Warden of the Closed Gate** · Fail-Safe Defaults · Abjuration

Zephyrine raised the ward-walls of the northern capital, and when the power failed and every ward in the city collapsed, they collapsed shut. Thousands lived because her magic's failure state was designed as carefully as its success state.

> Anything can fail. The only question you ever get to answer in advance is which way.

### Mirelle the Illusionist

**Weaver of the Willing Path** · Psychological Acceptability · Illusion

Mirelle spent a decade studying not spells but people, and concluded that the strongest ward in Cybersecuratia was the one everyone quietly walked around. Her illusions make the safe path look like the obvious path, so travellers take it without ever feeling handled.

> A control your people route around is not a control. It is a rumour.

### Osric the Diviner

**Keeper of the Unblinking Ledger** · Complete Mediation · Divination

Osric sees every request made anywhere in the realm — not the first one, every one. He learned the hard way, watching a checked-once gate be walked through nine thousand times, that a permission verified at the door means nothing an hour later.

> Check it again. Yes, even that one. Especially that one.

### Pell the Conjurer

**Summoner of Exactly Enough** · Least Privilege · Conjuration

Pell conjures precisely the servant a task requires, with precisely the reach that task needs, and dismisses it the moment the work is done. Other conjurers summoned things that were powerful, permanent and convenient; their cities are the ones GRIDFALL took first.

> Grant what the task needs. Not what the person asks for, and never what is easiest to grant.

### Lysbet the Enchanter

**Binder of the Double Oath** · Separation of Privilege · Enchantment

Lysbet's great vaults cannot be opened by Lysbet. Every seal she casts requires two willing minds and two separate keys, a design she adopted after a single trusted archmage emptied a treasury in an afternoon and was, technically, fully authorized to do so.

> One hand on the lever is one hand too few.

### Cobb the Transmuter

**Shaper of the Small Machine** · Economy of Mechanism · Transmutation

Cobb takes a spell of four hundred moving parts and returns it with nine, and the nine do the same work. He is not tidying for its own sake: he has read the incident reports, and complexity is where flaws go to hide from the people reviewing them.

> You cannot review what you cannot hold in your head. Make it smaller.

### Wick the Necromancer

**Warden of the Shared Grave** · Least Common Mechanism · Necromancy

Wick works with what refuses to stay buried — the shared cache, the common channel, the one resource every service in the realm quietly leans on. He isolates them, because the day a thing everyone depends on turns, it does not turn for one city. It turns for all of them.

> What you share with everyone, you share with the adversary too.

### Faye the Evoker

**Author of the Published Fire** · Open Design · Evocation

Faye printed her entire spellbook and nailed it to the academy door, then dared the continent to break a single working. Nobody has. Her power was never in the secrecy of the method — it is in the key she alone holds, and she will tell you the difference at length.

> If hiding how it works is what makes it work, it does not work.

## The Return

Eight mentors. Four colossi. Roughly five hundred questions standing between Cybersecuratia and the way it used to be. The wizards have taught everything they know to exactly nobody so far. Go and be the exception.
